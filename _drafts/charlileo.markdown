Seeing Through Galileo's Eyes

One evening, I saw Jupiter out of my bathroom window. I grabbed my heavy astrobinoculars, balanced them on the window, and took a closer look. I was delighted when I found it in my new magnified view. And then I saw them. A line of small dots. Like a REALLY straight line of dots going across Jupiter. Could it be? I immediately saw in my mind Galileo's sketches when he discovered the four Galilean moons, or what he called the Medician moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. Now I was seeing them too! What a thrill! I can only imagine how much more of a thrill it was for him being the first to ever see them. He had implemented many improved designs for what was back then called a "spyglass," a device billed as enabling scheduling for shipments by sea by seeing them coming from far away. Galileo significantly increased its magnifying ability and pointed upward to the sky rather than out to sea, and we now know it as the telescope.

I was so excited that I went and grabbed my tripod and set it up in my bathroom! They were even easier to see with a stable support. I decided to sketch the moons just as he did <link to his sketches>.

Since that night, I have not felt the same. It's hard to describe the feeling I had. I feel changed somehow. I feel a part of the great journey of human civilization to track and understand the sky. I sketched them again another time. The moons were in different places! There are things happening out there that I can witness unfolding. We are for sure not the center of everything.

<pic>

Certainly when Galileo saw what I saw he felt the same, though this realization carried much more weight back then. He was already aware of the dangers of speaking on things that were not centering the Earth in the conversation. He saw what happened to Copernicus, he did not want it to happen to him. So as he describes his discovery in his work Sidereus Nuncius, in telling the King of Medicina of his discovery, he makes no inferences, no declarations, only states what he saw, and allows any reader to make the obvious connections. It was also really interesting to me how Galileo presented his information. He didn't make any bold claims that would get him Copernicus-ed...he said look this is what I saw, and let the implications of what he saw speak for themselves. This was the first time something was clearly seen to orbit another body that was not Earth. At that time, the geocentric model of the solar system was still the predominant view. People thought that all the planets and ALSO THE SUN, revolved around Earth. Galileo's observations bolstered the heliocentric view by proving that not everything orbited Earth.

Our entire "world view" changed because of a tool that enabled us to see beyond what we could. Tools are important. There are so many tools today that enable us to do much more than what our 5 senses allow. This has happened many times in history. For example, when the director of the Hubble Space Telescope decided to focus his 10% of Director discretionary time to have the Hubble focus on a seemingly boring patch of sky for 10 consecutive days, thousands of galaxies were found there. When the Chandra X-ray observatory took a look at the Cat's Eye nebula, they found an inner shell that had never been seen before because it was emitting X-ray light, not optical (visible) light as we can see with our eyes and telescopes like Hubble. (ROYGBIV) The renowned James Webb telescope has shown us amazing findings and structures in the universe and it only see in infrared!

People often talk about seeing is believing, but humans are terrible at seeing things! The optical light we see everyday is a very small fraction of the electromagnetic spectrum and the faintness brightness we can see in the sky with our eyes is extremely bright in objective terms. So when Galileo came around talking about I see this and that, they didn't know how to handle it. It wasn't clear what it was "doing" to what they were seeing. Was it enabling them to see better or was it creating something that wasn't there? Understanding objective truth is not about seeing something with your own eyes, it's about making an observation with the right tool and the right process.

I appreciate how directly Galileo took credit for his discovery. He knows for sure he is the first to see this, with his new tool making it possible. We can all learn to take ownership of our successes as much as we are taught to take ownership of our failures.
